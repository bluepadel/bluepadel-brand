01

Where we are — and why it reads as clip-art

  • 1It’s literal. A racket drawn as a racket says “padel app.” It doesn’t say what makes us different — that we score the game no one else can.
  • 2One flat weight. A single white silhouette knocked out of a flat blue square. No hierarchy, no craft, nothing that survives being shrunk to a favicon or blown up on a court wall.
  • 3The tile is the default. A stock rounded-square is what every side-project ships. The container is doing no work.
  • 4Nothing is ownable. Swap the racket for any sport’s gear and the logo still “works” — which means it isn’t ours.

Five directions — safe evolution → bold departure

A

The Elevated Racket

Evolution · lowest risk
app
reversed
BluePadel

The idea

Keep the racket equity the brand already has — but treat it like a logo, not a sticker. Truer proportion, an open perf pattern, and a ball-yellow ball in play on the sweet spot: the one detail that says “a point, being scored.”

Why it works

Zero recognition risk — anyone who knows the app still knows it. The yellow ball introduces the scoring story and gives us an ownable accent. Reads cleanly reversed and as a line-mark.

The trade-off

Still fundamentally “a racket.” It’s a better version of today, not a re-position. If the goal is to stop looking like a padel app, this is the least of the five that gets us there.

B

The Perforation Monogram

Balanced · keeps padel DNA
app
reversed
BluePadel

The idea

Only padel rackets are perforated — so the holes, not the racket outline, are the real signature. Arrange them into a “P” built from the perforation grid, with a single ball-yellow hole as the live point.

Why it works

Abstract enough to be a proper monogram, specific enough that it could only be padel. It’s a system: the dot-grid becomes a texture for backgrounds, loading states, court graphics. Superb at favicon size.

The trade-off

A step away from instant “that’s the racket” recognition — it asks the viewer to read a letter first. Needs the wordmark alongside it for a season while it earns its meaning.

The Trajectory

Story-led · our differentiator
app
reversed

The idea

The mark is the product: a ball’s flight, tracked as a rising arc of points that resolve into one ball-yellow ball — the moment a shot becomes a score. The tracking dots double as the racket’s perforations.

Why it works

Nobody else in padel can honestly use this mark, because nobody else tracks the ball. It carries the slogan visually — “automatically scored” drawn, not just written — and animates beautifully in product and on the web.

The trade-off

The most conceptual of the five; it trades literal padel-recognition for meaning. Best paired with the wordmark until the story is known.

The Eye

Bold · tech-forward

reversed

The idea

Lean into the hardware that makes it real — the BluePadel Eye. A lens/aperture ring closing on a ball-yellow centre that reads as both an iris and the ball. The camera that never misses a point.

Why it works

Signals precision technology, not sports equipment — exactly the “not a startup” reposition. Distinctive silhouette, premium at any size, and it ties the brand to the patented sensing system rather than the racket.

The trade-off

Furthest from “padel” at a glance; leans on the wordmark to place the category. Risks reading generic-tech if the execution isn’t crisp.

E

Wordmark-led + “bp” monogram

Mature · type-forward
app
reversed
BluePadel Every game, automatically scored.

The idea

Let the name do the work. A confident BluePadel wordmark with a real weight contrast (light “Blue”, heavy “Padel”) and a single ball-yellow point as the full-stop. The icon shrinks to a tight “bp” monogram for app and avatar use.

Why it works

The most grown-up move — established brands lead with type. Nothing to “get,” instantly legible, and the yellow full-stop quietly plants the ball/point idea. Cheapest to apply consistently everywhere.

The trade-off

Least distinctive symbol — a wordmark needs a strong custom cut to avoid feeling plain, and gives us less to play with in tiny/icon-only spots than a bespoke mark.

A route through it

Shortlist C — Trajectory and B — Perforation Monogram.

C is the one that can only be ours: it draws the slogan the exec already loves. B is the safer sibling — unmistakably padel, endlessly systematic, and brilliant small. Both pair naturally with the E wordmark, so whichever symbol wins, the type system comes along.

If the board wants zero recognition risk this quarter, A ships in a week and still adds the scoring ball. Keep D — The Eye in reserve for the hardware/IP story.

1Pick one symbol direction to develop (my vote: C, with B as the hedge) — not a final logo, a direction to refine.
2Lock the wordmark (E) and the ball-yellow accent rule in parallel — that’s reusable under any symbol.
3Take the chosen direction to proper vector artwork + a one-page identity (clear-space, min-size, mono/reversed, app-icon, court-wall) before it touches the product or site.
